02 · TEAM THESIS

Team Thesis

Follow-ups on Requests for Information (RFIs) are driven substantially by clarity defects such as missing references/documents, quantities without units, double- barreled questions, or undefined abbreviations. With the use of an AI drafting assistant at the creation stage, we expect at least a 10% relative reduction, based on our project lens data, in sent RFIs with at least one defect.

One point where construction information flow breaks down is the moment an RFI is written. An issue can be noticed in the field by a subcontractor, translated into an RFI by a general contractor, and then sent to the respective party within the project. This means that the wording at the creation stage is what every later party has to work from.

RFIs should reference specific drawings, documents, and specifications, say who needs to respond, and include a needed-by date, because the initial RFI as well as the response become part of the official project record (PM Problems, 2020).

When one of these essential details of an RFI is missing, the responder has to ask for it before they can answer, which triggers a follow-up and at times multiple rounds of clarification (SubmittalLink, n.d.).

RFIs are also costly before any follow-up happens. A study found that the estimated average cost of an RFI is $1,080 to review and respond to, with a median response time of 9.7 days, and about 22% of RFIs never answered (Navigant Construction Forum, 2013).

Each follow-up adds another review and response cycle to that cost, and an RFI can go unanswered, adding delay to the project.

We’re focusing on the creation stage because most of these defects can be caught before the RFI is sent out using an AI drafting assistant by checking for units, expanding abbreviations, splitting double-barreled questions, and asking for drawing or specification references.

03 · PROJECT LENS

Project Lens

Our team will use the public RFI log from the Brush Creek Work Center Construction solicitation (127EAW26R0001), issued by the U.S. Forest Service. The log is dated March 27th, 2026 and it contains 271 questions from bidders and their respective government responses.

These are pre-award questions from bidders to the government, not construction-phase RFIs that have been passed from the subcontractors then through to a GC then to a design team. However, this log still fits our study.

Each of the questions is a real contractor's own wording regarding drawings, specifications and scope so it works as a representation of the original issue that a GC would formalize.

The government's response will also give us a reference answer to check whether a question that is rewritten would get a similar response.

Many entries have to do with proposal logic, like page limits and past performance. We can easily filter this to just technical questions and select approximately 10 technical RFIs that best align with our four demo conditions (listed below).

We do not plan to use synthetic data. If a test will require it, such as ambiguity, we will instead just modify a real RFI and keep its construction context.

04 · COORDINATION POSTURE

Coordination Posture

01

Subcontractor / Contractor

identifies the original construction issue and communicates the request for clarification.

→
02

General Contractor (GC)

reviews the issue, uses AI assistance to rewrite or structure the request, verifies that the original meaning is preserved, and submits the formal RFI.

→
03

Design Team / Owner Representative

receives the RFI, interprets the request, and provides clarification or a response.

Information artifacts in scope

The main information artifact is the Request for Information (RFI). Additional artifacts may include the original contractor question, relevant project drawings, specifications, and supporting project documents needed to interpret the RFI.

AI coordination posture

AI will be used only as a drafting and organization assistant for RFIs. It may improve clarity, grammar, structure, and conciseness, but the human user remains responsible for verifying the rewritten RFI before submission. AI should not change the original intent, introduce unsupported assumptions, resolve ambiguity on its own, or make design or scope decisions.

05 · DEMO CONCEPTS

Four Demo Concepts

Each demo targets one of the clarity defects identified in our thesis.

01

Missing Reference Detection

Test whether the AI drafting assistant can identify and prompt for missing drawing, document, or specification references before an RFI is sent.

02

Quantity and Unit Check

Test whether the AI drafting assistant can detect quantities that are missing units or otherwise unclear.

03

Bundled Question Detection

Test whether the AI drafting assistant can identify double-barreled questions and split them into clearer individual asks.

04

Undefined Abbreviation Check

Test whether the AI drafting assistant can identify undefined abbreviations and prompt the user to clarify or expand them.

06 · TEAM WEB PRESENCE

Team Web Presence
